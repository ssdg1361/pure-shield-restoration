## Older Boynton and West Boynton

Boynton Beach covers a lot of ground, and the water problems change as you head west.

A lot of the housing east of I-95 went up in the '60s and '70s. Leisureville and the older neighborhoods around it still run plenty of original plumbing, and those pipes tend to fail inside a wall where nobody sees it for a while. Closer to the water, the condos near the marina district and along Federal Highway share walls, ceilings and pipes with the units around them.

West Boynton is mostly gated communities like Aberdeen, Hunters Run and the Valencias. Many of the owners there live in Boynton part of the year, so a leak can run for months before anyone opens the door.

We're based in Boca, so Boynton is a short run up I-95. When you call, one of the two owners picks up, and the same two people who look at the job do the work.

## Original plumbing in the older neighborhoods

Supply lines and drains from the '60s and '70s rarely give out all at once. A fitting starts to weep or a drain corrodes from the inside, and the water soaks the bottom of a wall or the slab under the floor for months.

You'll usually notice small things first. A closet smells musty. A baseboard swells or pulls away from the wall. A spot in the floor feels soft, or the water bill goes up for no reason.

We find the wet area with thermal imaging and moisture meters before we open anything. That way we cut where the water went, and you keep as much of your drywall as the job allows.

## Seasonal homes in West Boynton

If you spend the summer up north, your house sits closed for months with the AC set high or turned off. A small drip under a sink or a clogged AC line has all that time to work, and mold grows fast in a closed-up Florida house.

We saw the humidity version of this in a Boynton home with no leak at all. The house sat closed up, moisture settled into every room where the air didn't move, and mold spread across the bedroom walls, up into the ceiling corners and around the AC vents. [Here's how we handled that job](/work/boynton-bedroom-mold/).

Before you leave, close the main water supply, leave the AC running, and have someone open the door once a month. Our [checklist for opening up a home that sat closed](/blog/snowbird-return-home-checklist-florida/) covers what to look at when you get back.

If something goes wrong while you're away, call us. We can work with your property manager, a neighbor or the association for access, send photos as we go, and talk it through with you before we do anything beyond stopping the damage.

## Condos near the marina and along Federal

In a condo, the leak that ruins your ceiling often starts in the unit above. A supply line, a water heater or a dishwasher connection fails upstairs, and the water follows the wall down into your space.

If it's happening right now:

- Shut off your water if there's any chance the source is yours, and tell the neighbor above and the association.
- Take photos before anything gets moved or cleaned up.
- Start the drying before everyone agrees on who pays. Wet drywall can start growing mold in a day or two.

Both insurers will want documentation, and we photograph and meter the damage the way adjusters expect. Our [insurance claims page](/services/insurance-claims/) covers what's usually covered and what usually isn't.

## AC closets and condensate lines

The AC runs nearly year round here, so the condensate line does too. When it clogs with algae and dust, the pan overflows into the closet wall, the floor or the ceiling below. It starts as a stain or a soft spot, and by the time you see it, it's usually been dripping for weeks.

Look at the wall or ceiling under your air handler once a month, and ask your AC tech to clear the drain line at every service.

## Storms and wind-driven rain

Tropical systems push water in around windows, doors and roofs, flood zone or not. Rain that blows in around a window is usually clean water, and if it's dried fast, most materials can be saved. Water that comes in from outside at ground level counts as flood, which means a different job and a different policy. [Flood vs. water damage](/blog/flood-vs-water-damage-florida-insurance-cost/) explains how that works.

If there's standing water, the first step is [emergency water extraction](/services/emergency-water-extraction/), then [structural drying](/services/structural-drying/) until the meters say dry.

## Testing is a separate call

Florida doesn't let the same company test for mold and remove it on the same job. We're licensed remediators, so when testing makes sense, an independent assessor does it, and we'll tell you when it's worth paying for.
