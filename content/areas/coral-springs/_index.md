## Family homes from the '70s to the '90s

Most of Coral Springs was built between the '70s and the '90s, and most of it is single-family homes. Neighborhoods like Eagle Trace, Turtle Run, Ramblewood and Maplewood are full of houses still running their original supply lines, valves and cast-iron drains.

That plumbing is reaching the age where failures get common. The damage usually starts inside a wall or under the slab, where you won't see it early.

We cover Coral Springs from our Boca base. When you call, one of the two owners picks up, and the same two people who look at the job do the work.

## Plumbing hitting its age

Old supply lines pinhole and old fittings weep. Cast-iron drains corrode from the inside. None of it makes noise, and the water can soak the bottom of a wall or the slab for months.

Watch for a musty smell that doesn't go away, a warm spot on the floor, a stain on the drywall, or a water bill that jumps for no reason. Any of those is worth a look, and the inspection is free.

We find the wet area with thermal imaging and moisture meters before we open anything. That way we cut where the water went, and you keep as much of your drywall as the job allows.

## Water heaters and appliances

The classic Coral Springs flood happens overnight. A 12-year-old water heater, a washing machine hose or a dishwasher line lets go in a family home and soaks three rooms before anyone's awake.

If it happens to you, shut off the water at the main, stay out of standing water near outlets, and take photos before anything gets moved. Then call. The first day of drying costs the least, and every day after that more material goes from "dry it" to "replace it."

A few cheap habits help. Replace rubber washer hoses with braided stainless, and know where your main shutoff is before you need it.

## Open floor plans spread the water

Newer, open-plan homes have their own problem. With big connected rooms and tile running wall to wall, water spreads along the floor and into the base of every wall it reaches.

We dried out a newer open-plan Coral Springs home after water got in and spread exactly that way. We sealed off the affected walls, wrapped the kitchen island in plastic, and ran ducted drying equipment to push dry air where the moisture was. Then we checked the attic framing and ductwork above, because missed water turns into a mold job six weeks later. [Here's how that job went](/work/coral-springs-structural-drying/).

That's [structural drying](/services/structural-drying/): equipment stays until the meters say dry, across floors, walls and attic.

## Slab and irrigation leaks

Slow slab leaks, and irrigation lines running against the foundation, wick moisture up into walls and flooring over months. You might see baseboards swelling on an outside wall or flooring that starts to lift near the patio door.

## AC condensate

Big family homes run the AC hard all year. When the condensate line clogs, the pan overflows into ceilings and closets, and it can drip for weeks before anyone notices.

Look at the wall or ceiling under your air handler once a month, and ask your AC tech to clear the drain line at every service.

## Storms and humidity inland

You don't need a beach to get storm damage. Storm season pushes water in around windows, doors and roof edges this far inland too. Rain that blows in around a window is usually clean water, and if it's dried fast, most materials can be saved. [Flood vs. water damage](/blog/flood-vs-water-damage-florida-insurance-cost/) explains when outside water becomes a flood claim.

Inland Broward has the same humidity as the coast. Closed rooms, closets and garages grow mold anywhere moisture hangs around.

If there's standing water, the first step is [emergency water extraction](/services/emergency-water-extraction/).
