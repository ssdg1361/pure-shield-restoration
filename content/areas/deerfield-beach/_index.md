## Century Village is its own kind of job

There are thousands of units in Century Village, most of them built in the 1970s, most of them stacked two or three high with shared plumbing running between them. So we see the same failure over and over: a supply line, water heater or drain lets go upstairs, and the damage lands in the unit below.

Compared to a house, the extra work here is the paperwork and the access. There are usually two insurers involved, sometimes three parties if the association's policy touches it, and a set of building rules about when crews can work, where equipment can sit and how the common areas have to be protected. The wet drywall still needs the same work, but the documentation has to be right from the first hour, because every one of those parties is going to ask for it.

We handle these buildings regularly. We follow the association's protocol, coordinate access with the property manager when the owner is away, and photograph and meter everything as we go so nobody is arguing the claim from memory six weeks later.

## The half-year problem

A large share of Deerfield lives here part of the year, and that changes almost every job we do in this city.

A leak in an occupied home gets caught in hours, because somebody hears it, smells it or steps in it. The same leak in a unit closed up from May to November runs for months. It's the same leak with months more time to soak in.

By the time someone opens the door in the fall, drywall that took on water in June has been sitting in a warm, humid, closed box all summer. That's close to ideal for mold, and it's why our fall calls so often turn out bigger than people expected.

If you close up a place here for the season, we wrote up what to check before you leave and the order to check things in when you get back: [what to check before you unpack](/blog/snowbird-return-home-checklist-florida/).

## Kitchen leaks hide behind the cabinets

The most common mold job we do in Deerfield is a kitchen, and it usually goes the same way. A supply line or drain behind the cabinets seeps slowly, so nothing drips onto the floor or pools. The water runs along the base of the wall behind the lower cabinets, out of sight, and mold grows in the wall cavity.

The signs are small: a musty smell that's strongest when you open the cabinet under the sink, a toe kick that's swollen or separating, a section of baseboard that's discolored at the bottom edge.

Fixing it means opening the wall behind the counter. Cleaning the surface won't reach it. [This Deerfield kitchen job](/work/deerfield-kitchen-mold/) shows the work start to finish, including what we found behind the cabinets.

## Bathrooms, attics, garages

### Bathrooms

Dark grout and discolored caulk are usually surface problems. The bigger question is whether water has gotten behind the tile: under a shower pan, around a tub surround, into the wall the vanity sits against. A surface cleaning and a structural remediation are very different jobs at very different prices, and you can't tell which one you have from the outside.

### Attics and air handlers

If the air handler is in the attic or a closet, keep an eye on the condensate line. A clogged line backs the pan up, and the water goes into the ceiling below. In South Florida, attic mold usually comes from the AC rather than the roof, so the fix covers the drain line as well as the drywall.

### Garages

Between poor door seals, a slab that wicks moisture and a second refrigerator running in the corner, garages in Broward stay damper than most people assume. Mold on the back wall or along the bottom of stored boxes is common, and it's fixable.

## Testing, estimates, and who does which

People ask us about mold testing a lot. In Florida, mold **assessment** (the testing, the air and surface samples, the written protocol) is a separate license from mold **remediation**, and in most cases one company can't do both on the same property. The law is set up that way so the company that finds the mold isn't also the one selling you the removal.

We're remediators, and we hold Florida mold remediation license MRSR5407. When a job needs independent testing or a clearance sample, we work alongside a third-party assessor, which keeps the two jobs separate the way the statute intends. The longer version, including how to check any Florida license in about a minute, is in [mold inspection vs. mold remediation](/blog/mold-inspection-vs-mold-remediation-florida/).

An estimate is a different thing from an assessment, and it's free. We come out, take moisture readings and tell you what the job is.

## Water mitigation, drying, and restoration

**Water mitigation** is the emergency half: [extracting standing water](/services/emergency-water-extraction/), stopping the spread, and getting the structure drying before the damage gets worse. That work happens within hours.

**[Structural drying](/services/structural-drying/)** is the part people underestimate. We run air movers and dehumidifiers until the moisture readings say the structure is dry, even after the surface feels dry. In this humidity that usually takes longer than anyone wants. If you cut it short, a well-extracted flood can turn into a mold job four months later.

**[Water damage restoration](/services/water-damage-restoration/)** covers everything from the first call until you can use the space again, and **[mold remediation](/services/mold-remediation/)** is for when mold has already started growing.

## The Cove, Deer Creek and the waterfront

Deerfield has plenty of single-family homes too. In Deer Creek and Crystal Lake the problems are slab plumbing, aging water heaters and AC condensate rather than shared risers.

On the Intracoastal side in The Cove, we keep seeing wind-driven rain get in around sliders, window frames and door thresholds during storm season. It comes in at the edges and runs into the wall, so it often shows up as a stain at the base of a wall well after the storm has passed.
