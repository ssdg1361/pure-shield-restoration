## The beach towers and their aging plumbing

Most of the residential stock along the A1A corridor in 33062 went up in the 1960s and 70s. Those buildings share vertical **risers**, the supply and drain lines that run floor to floor through stacked bathrooms and kitchens. Sixty years in, the fittings on those lines are older than anyone designed them to last.

When a riser fitting starts to weep, you won't see it at first. The water runs down inside the chase between units and shows up two or three floors below, in the ceiling of someone who has no leak of their own. By the time anyone sees a stain, the cavity has been damp long enough for mold to take hold.

The same thing happens on a smaller scale with leaks inside a unit, like a washing machine hose, a water heater, a toilet supply line or an ice maker line behind a refrigerator. In a single-family home that water spreads sideways across a slab. In a tower it goes down, and now it's your neighbor's problem as well as yours.

## When the water is coming from the unit above you

This is the most common call we get in Pompano, and it's the one people handle wrong most often.

Most people wait for the neighbor to fix their leak, for the association to weigh in, or for the adjuster to come look. Meanwhile the ceiling is holding water and the drywall stays wet. Mold typically starts growing within 24 to 48 hours of materials getting wet, long before the association's next board meeting.

Treat it as two separate questions. One is who pays. The other is whether the wet material dries before mold starts, and that one can't wait. We stop the damage first. We [extract the water](/services/emergency-water-extraction/), open what needs opening, and get air moving through the cavity. We document the whole thing as we go, so you can sort out who pays afterward with photographs, moisture readings and a written scope instead of memory.

Generally, your policy covers your unit and your neighbor's covers theirs, but the association's governing documents and the cause of the leak can both complicate that. Read [what your policy is likely to cover](/services/insurance-claims/) before you call anyone.

## Ceiling damage usually goes past the ceiling

A brown ring on a ceiling is only the part you can see. Water runs along framing, insulation and electrical penetrations, and it usually spreads well past the stain.

Before anything gets patched, the cavity above the drywall has to read dry on a moisture meter. Dry to the touch isn't enough. If you paint over a ceiling that still holds moisture, you get a few months and then a bigger repair. If the material stayed wet too long, some of it has to come out instead of being dried, depending on what it is and how long it sat. We'll tell you which one you're looking at, and we'd rather dry and save something than tear it out for the sake of a bigger invoice.

## Where mold turns up in a Pompano home

### Kitchens

Kitchen mold grows under the sink and behind the lower cabinets, fed by a supply line or drain that seeps instead of dripping. Nothing puddles, so nobody notices. The first sign is usually a smell, or a cabinet toe kick that's started to swell.

### Bathrooms

Stained grout and caulk are usually a surface problem you can clean. The bigger question is whether water has gotten past the tile: behind a shower pan, around a tub surround, into the wall a vanity backs onto. Those are two different jobs at very different prices.

### AC closets and air handlers

A clogged condensate line on an AC that runs all year is the most common answer to "where is this water coming from" in Broward. The pan overflows, the water goes into the ceiling below or the closet wall behind, and it happens again every time the unit runs. We see this all the time in the stacked condos around Palm Aire and Cypress Bend.

### Garages and closed-up rooms

In a humid house, mold grows wherever the air sits still: garages with a poor door seal, guest rooms nobody opens, closets on an exterior wall a few blocks from the ocean. Salt air keeps everything a little damper than it should be.

## Mitigation, remediation, restoration: which one you need

People use these words interchangeably, but they mean different things.

**Water mitigation** is the emergency work: stopping the spread, extracting standing water, and drying the structure so the damage stops getting worse. It happens in the first hours and days.

**[Mold remediation](/services/mold-remediation/)** is removing mold that's already growing, under containment, and treating what stays. It comes later.

**[Water damage restoration](/services/water-damage-restoration/)** is the whole job, from the emergency call through [structural drying](/services/structural-drying/) to a space that's put back.

If the water is still there, you need mitigation today. If the water is long gone and you have a smell or a stain, you need remediation. If you're not sure which, that's normal, and it's what the free inspection is for.
