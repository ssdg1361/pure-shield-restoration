## The towers on the beach were built for a different era of plumbing

Most of the residential stock along the A1A corridor in 33062 went up in the 1960s and 70s. Those buildings share vertical **risers** — the supply and drain lines that run floor to floor through stacked bathrooms and kitchens. Sixty years in, the fittings on those lines are past the age anyone designed them for.

When a riser fitting weeps, it doesn't announce itself. The water runs down inside the chase between units and shows up two or three floors below, in a ceiling belonging to someone who has no leak of their own. By the time anyone sees a stain, the cavity has been damp long enough for mold to take hold.

The same thing happens on a smaller scale with in-unit failures. A washing machine hose, a water heater, a toilet supply line, an ice maker line behind a refrigerator. In a single-family home that water spreads sideways across a slab. In a tower it goes down, and it becomes somebody else's problem along with yours.

## When the water is coming from the unit above you

This is the most common call we get in Pompano, and it's the one people handle wrong most often.

The instinct is to wait — for the neighbor to fix their leak, for the association to weigh in, for the adjuster to look at it. Meanwhile the ceiling is holding water and the drywall is staying wet. Mold does not wait for the association's next board meeting. Growth typically starts inside 24 to 48 hours of materials getting wet.

What actually needs to happen is that the two questions get separated. Who pays is one question. Whether the wet material dries before it grows mold is a different question, and it has a much shorter clock. We stop the damage first — [extract the water](/services/emergency-water-extraction/), open what needs opening, and get air moving through the cavity — and document the whole thing as we go, so the payment question can be sorted out afterward with photographs, moisture readings and a written scope instead of memory.

Generally speaking, your policy covers your unit and your neighbor's covers theirs, but the association's governing documents and the actual cause of the leak both complicate that. It is worth reading [what your policy is likely to cover](/services/insurance-claims/) before you call anyone.

## Ceiling damage is almost never just the ceiling

A brown ring on a ceiling is the visible end of something that traveled. Water follows framing, insulation and electrical penetrations, and it usually spreads well past the stain.

Before anything gets patched, the cavity above the drywall has to be dry — actually dry, measured with a meter, not dry to the touch. Painting over a ceiling that still holds moisture buys a few months and a bigger repair. If the material stayed wet long enough, some of it comes out rather than gets dried; that call depends on what the material is and how long it sat. We'll tell you which one you're looking at, and we'd rather dry and save something than tear it out for the sake of a bigger invoice.

## Where mold actually turns up in a Pompano home

### Kitchens

Under the sink and behind the lower cabinets, fed by a supply line or a drain that seeps rather than drips. Nothing puddles, so nothing gets noticed. The first sign is usually a smell, or a cabinet toe kick that has started to swell.

### Bathrooms

Grout and caulk discoloration is usually surface and usually cleanable. What matters is whether water has gotten past the tile — behind a shower pan, around a tub surround, into the wall a vanity backs onto. Those are two different jobs and they cost very different amounts.

### AC closets and air handlers

Year-round cooling plus a clogged condensate line is the single most common source of "where is this water coming from" in Broward. The pan overflows, the water goes into the ceiling below or the closet wall behind, and it repeats every time the unit runs. We see this constantly in the stacked condos around Palm Aire and Cypress Bend.

### Garages and closed-up rooms

Anywhere air sits still in a humid house, mold finds it. Garages with a poor door seal, guest rooms nobody opens, closets on an exterior wall a few blocks from the ocean. Salt air keeps everything a little damper than it should be.

## Mitigation, remediation, restoration — which one you actually need

These get used interchangeably and they mean different things.

**Water mitigation** is the emergency work: stopping the spread, extracting standing water, and drying the structure so damage stops getting worse. It happens in the first hours and days.

**[Mold remediation](/services/mold-remediation/)** is removing growth that has already established, under containment, and treating what stays. It happens after the fact.

**[Water damage restoration](/services/water-damage-restoration/)** is the whole arc, from the emergency call through [structural drying](/services/structural-drying/) to a space that's put back.

If the water is still there, you need mitigation today. If the water is long gone and you have a smell or a stain, you need remediation. If you're not sure which, that's a normal thing not to know, and it's what the free inspection is for.
