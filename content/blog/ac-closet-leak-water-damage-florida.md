Most South Florida homes and condos keep the air handler in a closet, and the AC runs almost every day of the year. That makes the AC closet one of the most common places water shows up in a house.

It almost never shows up as a puddle you'd call someone about. It shows up as a soft baseboard, a musty hallway or a floor board that won't lie flat.

## Why AC closets leak

Your AC pulls water out of the air while it cools. That water collects in a pan under the unit and runs out through a small drain line. Here, that line runs nearly all year.

When the line clogs with algae and dust, the pan fills and spills over. Many units have a float switch that shuts the AC off before that happens, but not every unit has one, and the ones that do don't always work.

When the pan spills, the water doesn't stay in the closet. In most closets the air handler sits on a raised platform. The water runs down inside that platform onto the slab, then out under the walls and under whatever flooring is next to the closet.

## What it does to the wall and the floor

The water sits at the bottom of the wall, behind the baseboard, where nobody looks. Drywall wicks it up. Mold starts on the back of the baseboard and the bottom edge of the drywall long before anything shows on the painted side.

Floors take it next. Tile usually survives, but the grout and the slab underneath stay wet. Wood and laminate cup and lift. Hardwood glued to the slab can sit on wet adhesive for weeks.

We saw exactly this in [a west Boca home](/work/boca-ac-closet-leak-hardwood-mold/). The homeowner noticed a musty smell and some paint lifting by the closet. Our meter maxed out a few feet down the hall. Behind the baseboards there was mold, and the hardwood next to the closet had to come up.

## Signs to look for

- Water or rust stains in the pan or on the closet floor
- Paint bubbling or lifting at the bottom of the closet wall or the wall next to it
- A baseboard near the closet that's soft, swollen or pulling away
- A musty smell in the closet or the hallway
- Floor boards near the closet cupping, lifting or sounding hollow
- The AC shutting itself off for no clear reason
- A stain on the ceiling of the unit or room below

## A 2-minute monthly check

1. Open the closet and look at the pan under the unit. A little water while the AC runs is normal. A full pan isn't.
2. Look at the floor and the bottom of the walls inside the closet for stains, rust or soft spots.
3. Press on the baseboard on the other side of the closet wall. It should feel hard.
4. Smell the closet. Musty means moisture somewhere.
5. Ask your AC tech to clear the drain line and test the float switch at every service.
