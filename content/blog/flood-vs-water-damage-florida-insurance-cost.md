Water on your floor looks the same whether it came in through the door or up from a broken pipe. Where it came from decides who pays for it, and whether you need flood restoration or a standard water damage cleanup. Most South Florida homeowners learn the difference with the water already in the house.

## Flood water and water damage are two different claims

A burst pipe or a failed water heater is sudden water from inside the house, and a standard homeowners policy usually covers it. Water that rises from outside, from storm surge, heavy rain or a canal over its banks, counts as flood, and standard homeowners policies generally exclude it.

You buy flood coverage separately, through the National Flood Insurance Program or a private flood insurer. From the driveway the cleanup looks about the same. The claim goes to a different place, though, and the work inside is a bigger job than drying out a burst pipe.

## Why flood restoration is a bigger job

Restoration crews treat flood water as contaminated from the moment it comes in, however clear it looks. The industry standard we work to, IICRC S500, sorts water into three categories. Category 1 is clean water from a supply line. Category 2 carries some contamination, like a washing machine overflow.

Category 3 is grossly contaminated. Ground surface water and storm surge fall there by definition, because on the way to your house they pick up whatever is in the street, the soil, and the septic and sewer lines.

That category changes what we do on the job:

- **Porous materials come out.** We remove carpet, pad, insulation and the drywall below the water line, because nobody can disinfect the inside of them.
- **We seal off the work area.** Plastic barriers and negative air keep contamination from spreading through the house on shoes and airflow.
- **We disinfect what stays.** Surfaces get cleaned and treated before anything gets closed back up.
- **Meters decide when it's dry.** A wall can feel dry to your hand and still hold enough moisture for mold to start.

## What flood restoration costs in South Florida

Nobody can quote a flood job honestly without seeing it, and the size of the puddle matters less than you'd think. These four things move the number most:

- **How long the water sat.** The first day costs the least. In South Florida humidity, mold can start within 24 to 48 hours, and each day after that moves more of your house from "dry it and keep it" to "tear it out and replace it."
- **How much has to come out.** Cutting two feet of drywall around a room is a smaller job than cutting four feet and pulling the cabinets.
- **How far the water traveled.** Water runs under flooring and inside wall cavities. One wet room often turns out to be three, and moisture mapping is how we find the other two.
- **How long the house takes to dry.** Most jobs need a few days of monitored drying. Our humidity can stretch that out, so the equipment stays until the readings say dry.

Be wary of a firm price over the phone before anyone has taken a reading. That number is padded to cover what they can't see, or it's going to grow later.

## Florida law separates mold testing from mold removal

In Florida, the company that tests your home for mold generally can't be the company that removes it. Under Florida Statute 468.8419, a licensed mold assessor may not remediate a structure they assessed within the last twelve months, and a remediator may not assess a structure they remediated in that window.

The rule takes away any reason to find more mold than is there. We explain how it works, and the one exception, in [mold inspection vs. mold remediation in Florida](/blog/mold-inspection-vs-mold-remediation-florida/).

It also gives you an easy question for any company that shows up: are you the assessor or the remediator? Pure Shield is a licensed mold remediator, license number MRSR5407. We don't do our own clearance testing. When a job needs independent verification, a third party with no stake in the size of the job handles it.

## How to choose a flood restoration company

![Restoration technician in a full protective suit and respirator working in a contaminated crawlspace](/images/category-3-water-protective-equipment.jpg)
*Category 3 water calls for a full suit and a respirator. A shop vac won't cover it.*

Answering the phone at 2am is a good start, but check a few more things before you let a crew in. Ask for the Florida license number and look it up. Ask whether you'll get moisture readings and photos set up for an adjuster, since a claim without documentation turns into an argument later.

Ask who will be on site. With the big franchises, the estimator you meet often isn't on the crew that shows up. And ask what doesn't need doing. A company that won't say no to any part of the job is trying to sell you all of it.

## What to do first if flood water is in your house

Shut off the source if you can reach it safely, and stay out of standing water where the power may be live. Photograph everything before anything moves, because your adjuster will want those photos. Then call for help.

Have these ready when you call your insurer. They keep a claim moving:

- **When it started and what caused it.** The adjuster wants to know whether it was sudden or gradual, and the answer decides your coverage.
- **Where the water came from.** Inside the house or in from outside. That's the flood question, and it decides which policy pays.
- **Photos and video from before anything was moved or torn out.** Take more than you think you need, including inside cabinets and under furniture.
- **Your policy number and whether you have separate flood coverage.** If you're not sure, ask your agent.
