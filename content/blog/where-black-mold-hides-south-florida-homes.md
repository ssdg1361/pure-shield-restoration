## Where black mold hides: room by room

### Kitchens and cabinetry

The most common sign of a mold issue in a kitchen is a persistent musty smell near the lower cabinets, rather than obvious visible growth on the doors or walls. We see this exact scenario frequently during our [mold removal in Deerfield Beach](/areas/deerfield-beach/). On one recent job detailed in our [Deerfield Beach kitchen mold](/work/deerfield-kitchen-mold/) records, the root cause was a slow leak running along the back of the kitchen cabinet line. Water never dripped onto the floor, and nothing ever pooled where anyone could see it. Instead, the moisture slowly wicked into the drywall and fed microbial growth entirely inside the wall cavity behind the lower cabinets.

Handling a situation like this means opening up the wall behind the counter to reach the actual source of the problem. You cannot just wipe down the visible surfaces and hope the smell goes away. On that project, we protected the floors and walkways, removed the affected drywall and porous cabinet materials, and treated the cavity. We also extracted the moisture and dried the area as we went, ensuring the water left the house along with the damaged materials. The kitchen was completely dried and verified before any finishes were closed back up.

### Attics and ceilings

The wet spot you see on your ceiling is usually not the full extent of the damage, but just the small part of the damage that finally soaked through to the paint. In our region, many homes are two-story properties where the air handler sits in the attic above finished ceilings. We recently completed a project involving ceiling mold from an attic AC leak in Parkland, documented in our [Parkland attic AC leak](/work/parkland-attic-ac-leak-ceiling-mold/) case file. The issue started with just a small water stain over a bathroom, right beside a recessed light. AC condensate drain lines in the attic rarely burst. Instead, they clog with algae and dust, or a PVC fitting weeps slowly. Water leaves the drain pan a little at a time and sits on the back of the ceiling in a warm, dark, and humid environment.

On this job, we started our assessment with a moisture meter instead of a saw. The moisture readings ran far past the boundaries of the visible stain. We made small exploratory ceiling cuts before committing to large removal. This exploratory step is the one most often skipped by untrained workers, and skipping it is exactly how wet ceilings get patched over active growth. We found growth on the back of the ceiling stretching across an area much larger than the stain, which had carried up into the exposed wood framing and left the insulation wet. We removed the affected ceiling material because porous drywall cannot be cleaned once roots take hold. We HEPA vacuumed the framing and mechanically sanded the wood. Framing can usually be saved, but it must actually be cleaned, not just sprayed with chemicals. After treating and verifying the wood was dry, we sealed the framing and installed temporary poly sheeting until the rebuild could begin.

### Closets and spare rooms

Coastal humidity easily feeds microbial growth in closed-off spaces where your air conditioning does not circulate well. In South Florida, spare bedrooms and guest closets are often kept shut for weeks at a time. These are rooms nobody walks through daily. The air grows stagnant, humidity rises, and small roof leaks or window seal failures have plenty of time to spread moisture. By the time you open the closet door and spot dark growth on your clothes or the drywall, the environment has been damp for a long time. Maintaining proper airflow and checking these quiet rooms regularly is vital for catching water intrusion early.

## How to safely clean small surface spots

You can safely clean small patches of surface mold on hard materials using a simple mix of household detergent and warm water. If you find a tiny spot of mold on bathroom tile, shower glass, or metal hardware, you do not need to panic. The EPA notes that areas measuring under roughly ten square feet on hard, non-porous surfaces can usually be handled safely by a homeowner without professional help.

Before you begin cleaning, you must protect yourself. Wear an N95 respirator, thick rubber gloves, and safety goggles that do not have ventilation holes. Scrub the hard surface thoroughly with the detergent and water mixture, and allow it to dry completely. A major mistake homeowners make is assuming highly aggressive chemicals are required for cleanup. The EPA does not recommend bleach as a routine practice. If you do choose to use bleach on your bathroom tile, you must absolutely never mix it with ammonia or other household cleaners, as this creates highly dangerous fumes. Another critical rule is to never paint or caulk over mold. Paint will just peel, and the growth will continue eating through the material underneath.

## Florida licensing rules for mold remediation

Any mold removal project larger than ten square feet in the state of Florida requires a licensed professional to perform the work legally. The state regulates this industry heavily to protect homeowners from improper practices. You should always verify a contractor's Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation (DBPR) mold license number before allowing anyone to cut into your walls.
