The homeowner called about **a musty smell in the hallway** and paint lifting at the bottom of the wall next to the AC closet. Nothing was dripping and the floor looked fine.

The air handler in this **west Boca** home sits in a hall closet on a raised platform, the way a lot of single-story Boca homes are built. When the **condensate drain line clogged**, the pan under the unit overflowed. The water went down inside the platform, onto the slab and out under the walls and the floor. It had been doing that for weeks.

## What we found

We started with a **moisture meter**. On the drywall a few feet from the closet it maxed out, with the reading at the top of the wet range. That told us the water had traveled well past the closet before we cut anything.

Next we cut a **small inspection hole** low in the wall to look inside before doing anything bigger. Behind it were metal studs and wet drywall.

When the **baseboards came off**, the bottom edge of the drywall had mold along it, and the backs of the baseboards had it too. You couldn't see any of that from the hallway. Inside the platform under the air handler, the **slab was still wet** and stained, with debris sitting in the water.

The floor was **solid hardwood glued down to the slab**. When we pulled up the boards along the wet area, the adhesive underneath was still wet. Wood on a wet slab cups and lifts, and mold grows in the glue line where nobody can see it, so those boards had to come up.

## How we fixed it

We **cut away the bottom of the drywall** where the mold was and took the baseboards and the wet hardwood out of the closet and hallway. The metal studs and the slab got **HEPA vacuumed and treated with antimicrobial**. A **HEPA air scrubber** ran in the hallway the whole time.

Then we dried it. We **opened the AC platform** down to the studs and put an **air mover** inside the cavity, where the water had sat the longest. A **commercial LGR dehumidifier** ran in the hall. We took **readings every day** and left the equipment until the walls and the slab read dry.

Before the equipment came out, the **drain line got cleared**, so the same water wouldn't come back.

## Why this happens in Boca

A lot of Boca homes, especially single-story houses west of I-95, keep the air handler in a hall closet. The AC runs almost every day of the year, so the drain line does too, and it clogs with algae and dust. When it does, the water goes into the closet wall and the floor, and in a house with hardwood it can sit under the boards for weeks.

If you have a stain or a soft spot at the bottom of your AC closet wall, assume it goes further than it looks. Look at the wall under your air handler once a month, and ask your AC tech to clear the drain line at every service.
