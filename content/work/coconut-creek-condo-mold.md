The mold in this **Coconut Creek condo** ran along the bottom of the walls in a band a few inches high, in room after room. That pattern told us the water was coming from below. It was moving along the slab and wicking up into the drywall, and it had been doing it for a while.

The source was a **failed supply line**. That's usually an [emergency water extraction](/services/emergency-water-extraction/) call, but this one wasn't, and this kind of failure does more damage.

When a line lets go all at once, you get standing water, and somebody calls that same hour. When a line seeps, nothing pools and nothing drips where you can see it. The water moves along the slab month after month, soaking the bottom of every wall it reaches and feeding mold behind them while the rooms look normal. Nobody calls, because as far as anyone can tell there's nothing to call about.

## What we found

The staining you could see from the room was the smallest part of it.

Our moisture meter read off the top of its scale on wood that looked fine. When we pulled the baseboards, the backs of them were covered. Under the plank flooring there was growth beneath the underlayment, where nobody would think to look. And once we opened the wall, the hidden side of the drywall was far worse than anything showing on the finished side.

It had also traveled. Water from a supply line follows the slab, so by the time we finished tracing it, the affected area ran from the **living room through to the laundry**. That's the kind of spread people miss when they only treat the room where the stain showed up.

## How we remediated it

Disturbing mold spreads it, so we didn't remove anything until the area was sealed. We built **containment** with poly sheeting, taped it off at the openings, and ran a **HEPA air scrubber** the whole time we worked to pull spores out of the air before they could drift into the rest of the unit.

Then we cut the drywall back past the moisture line, which ran further than the staining, and removed the baseboards and the affected underlayment. You can't wipe porous materials clean once mold has rooted into them, so they have to come out. Everything that stayed got HEPA vacuumed and treated.

After that we started drying. We set up a dehumidifier and air movers and left them running until the structure **read dry on a meter**. A wall can look dry well before it reads dry, and in a South Florida slab building that gap decides whether the mold comes back in six months.

Someone was living in the condo the whole time. We kept the work inside the containment, and the rest of the unit stayed usable.

## Why this happens in Coconut Creek

Most of Coconut Creek's condos went up on slab between the 1970s and the 1990s, and the original supply lines are still in the walls. Those lines are decades old now. When one lets go behind drywall in a ground-floor unit, the water has nowhere to go but sideways along the slab and up into everything it touches. Owners often have no idea until a baseboard goes soft or the room starts to smell.

If you're in a condo here and notice staining low on a wall, a soft baseboard, or a musty smell you can't place, the problem is almost always below and behind what you can see.
